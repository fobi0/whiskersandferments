Hey :) we are back with something fermented (for a website with "ferment" in the name, there's not as much as expected, right?). This time, we are making a super simple ferment, but to this day, it's one of my all-time favorites. Living in Germany, you kinda grow up with sauerkraut, but nobody ever served me sauerkraut made from red cabbage. It's way sweeter, has a more interesting flavor profile, and can be adjusted to your needs. I often make spicy versions by adding some chilies and red onions to make it even more red.

I add it to everything... pasta dishes, salads, charcuterie boards, tacos... literally everything. I prefer eating it cold, but it's also pretty nice as a hot ingredient.

| Ingredient | Amount | Example | Step |
|------------|--------|---------|------|
| Red cabbage | 100% | 500g | Use a mandoline or a sharp knife and cut it into small slices - as thin as possible. |
| Salt | 2% | 10g | Add it to the cabbage. Now comes the annoying part: you have to press the liquid out of the cabbage. Press it with your hands (I recommend using gloves if you don't want to look like you killed someone afterwards) until some liquid forms at the bottom of the bowl. |
| | | | Put everything in a glass jar and press it tightly so that the liquid completely covers the cabbage. This is important to prevent mold from forming on the surface. You can add some weights on top to prevent the cabbage from floating. |
| | | | Now the hard part: you have to wait. Depending on your room temperature, it should take around 5 to 7 days until the lactic acid bacteria have eaten enough of the sugars and created a sour brine, which makes this dish safe to eat for weeks or even months. Taste the cabbage; if it's sour and tangy, it's done. Keep it in your fridge to prevent further fermentation and add it to everything! |
